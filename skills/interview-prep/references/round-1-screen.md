# Round 1: the recruiter screen

**What this round is.** A filter, not an evaluation. A recruiter is checking that the paperwork
is true, that the logistics work, that the money is in range, and that the candidate can hold a
conversation. Almost nobody is hired because of a screen. Plenty of people are eliminated in one,
usually on something that had nothing to do with their ability.

**Its second job is intelligence.** The recruiter will answer questions the hiring manager will
not: the band, the level ladder, who the manager is, how many rounds there are, what the team is
actually struggling with. Round 2 is prepped from what is collected here, so the questions asked
in this call are not politeness. They are the input to the rest of the process.

## Sections to write

### The gates

Open with the things that can end the process, each with the sentence to say. Cover every one
that applies; drop the ones that do not, silently, rather than writing "N/A".

- **Work authorization.** The single most common quiet rejection. If the user needs sponsorship
  or a permit for the role's country, write the question they ask **in the first three minutes**,
  and name every route that exists rather than making an open-ended request. A question shaped
  "here are two ways this works, which fits you" is answerable; "would you sponsor me" is a
  favour. Include the redirect if the answer is no.
- **Compensation.** Ask for their band first; it costs nothing and they usually answer. Have a
  number ready for when they push. Anchor on **the market the job is in**, not the user's last
  number if it came from a different market. Do not volunteer a pay cut.
- **Level and title.** If the posting's level is a step down or a stretch from the user's last
  role, they will raise it. Write the honest answer that neither disparages the level nor
  pretends it was not noticed, plus the question about how their ladder maps.
- **Why you left, or why you are leaving.** Two sentences, factual, no bitterness, then move
  forward. Layoff, resignation, still employed: whichever it is, it gets a short true version
  that does not become the theme of the call.
- **Availability and notice**, and relocation timing if the role is in another country.

### The two-minute pitch

"Tell me about yourself" is asked in essentially every screen and is the one answer worth having
word for word. Write it out, at speaking length, and structure it:

1. What the user is now, in one sentence, with the scale attached.
2. The thing that makes them non-generic for **this** posting.
3. One result with a number, from an atom.
4. Why this role, drawn from the analysis, in their vocabulary.

Then say what the pitch is doing, so the user can adapt it live rather than reciting it.

### Landmines

Anything true, discoverable, and awkward. Two or three at most. A competitor on the resume, a
product of theirs the user churned off, a short tenure, a second application at the same company,
a gap in the dates. For each: the straight answer, in one or two sentences. These are not
confessions, they are the difference between a smooth answer and an improvised one.

### Time budget

Any call under an hour gets a table, and a line saying what gets cut when they run long.

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | Pleasantries, then the gate question |
| 3 to 6 | The pitch, once, tightly |
| 6 to 25 | Their questions. Let them run the middle |
| 25 to 35 | Your questions, in priority order |
| 35 to 40 | Band and next steps, both asked explicitly |

### Questions to ask them

Priority ordered, and expect to get through half. Mark the gates. The standing set:

1. The work-authorization gate, if it applies, and **when in the process it gets confirmed**.
2. The band for this level in this location.
3. What the team is actually trying to get right over the next year.
4. Who the hiring manager is, and what they care about.
5. What the rest of the process looks like: how many rounds, who is in each, what each covers.
6. What the work model actually means in days per week.

**Question 5 is the one this whole flow depends on.** It converts a guess about rounds 2 and 3
into a fact, and it is the question candidates most often forget to ask.

## What to collect for round 2

Write these as an explicit checklist at the end of the file, because they are what makes the next
prep possible:

- The hiring manager's name, title, and how long they have been there.
- The real team size, shape, and what the open headcount is for.
- The stated format and topics of rounds 2 and 3.
- The band, and how it is split between base and equity.
- Any requirement the recruiter emphasised that the posting underplayed, or ignored entirely.
- Whatever the recruiter said the team's hard problem is, in their own words.
